Write a childhood memory. Find your door.

— the song —

In 1973, Bob Dylan wrote "Knockin' on Heaven's Door" for a single scene in Sam Peckinpah's film Pat Garrett & Billy the Kid. A sheriff, shot and bleeding in the dust of the American West, asks his wife to take his badge. He can't use it anymore. The song was supposed to be small — a moment in a film. Instead it became one of the most covered songs in history, because it names something almost everyone has felt: not death, but the laying down. The quiet moment when something you carried for years — a self, a summer, a version of home — stops belonging to you.

— what we do —

You write a memory. A small one is enough. The smell of a kitchen, a game played until the streetlights came on, a voice calling you in from outside. Something you didn't know you were leaving, in the moment you left it.

Two AI systems listen: one writes a poem for your memory — in the spare, aching voice Dylan used in 1973, when the counterculture was tired and tender and learning to let go. The other builds an image: your door, rendered in the warm grain of a 35mm photograph from that same year.

The poem and the image are yours. But they're also added to the wall — a growing collage of every door knocked here, every childhood left at the threshold. Someone you'll never meet wrote their memory an hour ago. It's waiting for you in the gallery.

— our door —

This project was built on the feeling that childhood ends the way a badge gets laid down: not in a ceremony, not with a goodbye, but in a slow realization that it already happened. Time moves without asking. One summer you're running barefoot on warm asphalt and someone is calling your name from a window — and then, years later, you understand that was the last time. You just didn't know it yet.

We built KNOCK because we believe those moments deserve to be held still. Even for a minute. Even in a poem and a faded photograph. Because knocking on heaven's door isn't about what's behind it — it's about who you were when you knocked.
